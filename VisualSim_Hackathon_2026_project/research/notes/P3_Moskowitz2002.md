# A Comparison of Tactical Ballistic Missile Defense Engagement Coordination Schemes (Moskowitz, Gassler, Paulhamus, 2002, JHU APL Technical Digest 23(2-3), pp.272-285)
- File: `VisualSim_Hackathon_2026_project/research/P3_Moskowitz2002.pdf` + `.txt` (14 pages). Markings: none. ACES v0.9. Funded ONR + Navy Theater Wide program office.

## Problem & motivation
- Future TBMs in massive advanced raids will overwhelm individual units; unprecedented synergy among dissimilar units required; unit-centric doctrine impedes it.
- Shooter-selection today (unbidded first-launch + unique asset assignment) only deconflicts "free fire"; past analysis preferred distributed engagement decision (DED) preferred-shooter [P3, p.1].
- Beyond avoiding overengagement: minimize leakers by minimizing free riders + maximizing depth of fire (salvos separated by kill assessment) + picking highest-Psuccess engagements. Area TBMD rarely allows reengagement after assessment [P3, p.1].
- Uses ACES to contrast schemes under ideal vs achievable configs, comparing raid-outcome distributions [P3, p.1].

## Threat/mission context
- Theater area TBMD vs TBM raid: 20 TBMs from 5 launch areas (15 Scud C + 5 No Dong, RVs separating post-burnout); multi-object [P3, p.6].
- 29-entry defended-asset list; 10 defendable from all areas by >=1 ship; 7 near aimpoints (Table 2: Asset 6: T20; 7: T1,3-5,17; 12: T13,14,16; 13: T12; 15: T11; 20: T7,8,19; 24: T2,6,9,10,15,18; 8/22/25: none) [P3, p.6].
- Endo-atmospheric intercept window; shoot-look-shoot effectively unavailable (volume + speed) [P3, p.1]. C2 problem: who shoots which/when under imperfect picture + limited comms.

## Defense layer / system element
- Aegis area TBMD only (v0.9 limit; other types/AAW/OCMD future). 4 ships at COEA-based locations; not all threats engageable by all (0 by all 4; 9 by 3; 7 by 2; 4 by 1) [P3, p.6, Fig.2].
- Weapon: SM-2 Block IVA only; dual salvos; policy one salvo per threat; 2nd shot not scheduled until 1st in flight -> one scheduled shot per threat [P3, p.4].
- Radar AN/SPY-1B(V)/D; Baseline 6 Ph 3 threat-assessment/engageability/scheduling + special auto-TBMD reaction [P3, p.4]. THAAD/Patriot/NTW/fighters/E-2C/E-3A/C2 only size Link-16 net, not shooters [P3, p.6].

## Method / approach
- ACES v0.9: unit-level decision/control/execution in multi-sensor/weapon/target env; sensor/weapon flow + info exchange at fidelity to evaluate automated coordination + force effectiveness. 1-s periodic threat-assessment/engageability/scheduling/force-selection. Priority: (1) preferred shooter, (2) threat priority, (3) earliest latest-launch. Max 5 simultaneous discriminations per platform (ORD threshold/objective) [P3, pp.2-4].
- Free fire (no coordination): independent firing at first-appearing threats; expends radar, overengages few while later become free riders; worsens with overlap [P3, p.2].
- Simple sectored (unbidded static, no comms): assets partitioned uniquely; only engage own assets. Pro: simple, ~zero overengagement (only overlapping impact areas or disagreed predictions). Con: uneven raid overwhelms one unit while neighbor idle [P3, p.2].
- First launch (unbidded dynamic, light comms): independent plans; defer once launch message received; "engagement terminated" on failure allows reschedule. Network-free variant by observing launches not used here. Pro: fewer free riders vs sectored, easy. Con: capability-blind (farthest-first due to TOF in small endo window, lowest Psuccess, consumes resources leaving unique threats free); latency-sensitive; identical TOF contours -> overengagement regardless of speed; dual tracks -> overengagement; miscorrelation -> free riders [P3, pp.2-3].
- DED (bidded dynamic, heavy net): share possible-engagement info, run common algorithm to synchronized assignment; defer if not preferred. FES (Force Engagement Schedule) per unit: entries = threat + unit + weapon + shot (max 2 per unit-threat with dual salvos). Temp hierarchical DB: group -> threat -> possible shot; spatially close threats grouped (threshold 1000 m); every threat exactly one group; frozen when within min coordination time of launch; ordered by frozen/size/uniquely-capable/priority/number. Preferred-shot sieving 8 tests in order: (1) notification time, (2) hard capacity 5, (3) inventory sufficiency, (4) load balance soft 3 (restore if any threat emptied), (5) reengagement (allow intercept+assessment+coordination; drop units without; restore if emptied), (6) inventory balance (tolerance 3), (7) TOF sum tie-break, (8) unit number tie-break [P3, pp.3-6, Table 1: coord 4.375s ideal / 12.25s achievable, soft 3, hard 5, tolerance 3, group 1000 m; Fig.1 example].
- Ideal vs achievable: ideal = hemispheric max waveform, unlimited msgs/slot, no errors, perfect correlation, effective Pk=1. Achievable = just-in-time sectors, Swerling IV aspect RCS, noise+biases, limited Link-16 (2 msgs/slot), probabilistic kill chain [P3, pp.5-8].
- Link-16-like (Navy Network 021A; CEC AAW-only era): 20 C2 + 36 fighters sized; surveillance >=1/1.5s (THAAD 18 slots, Patriot ICC 30, others 8 each); engagement coordination 14 slots/shooter -> 1/0.875s; J3.6 space track + J3.0 impact + enhanced engagement status (J10.2I/C2) for DED [P3, p.6, Fig.3].
- Air picture: RCS planning via RP&A extended mission (goal 95% track init >=20s before special auto-TBMD reaction, constant-mean lethal Swerling IV) vs ACES roll/frequency-averaged VV aspect Swerling IV means (believed more accurate); APL Guaranteed Useful Search sectors for planned pairs only (ideal ignores reality); launch counting named but method unstated; ground-truth clustering now (RV+booster; future Baseline 6 Ph 3 local); POT via Baseline 6 Ph 3 (+deferred/preferred flags; remote-track future); 3-sigma impact ellipse -> highest-priority asset in ellipse; engageability via drag-ignored projection through downrange-vs-altitude hexagon by cross-range/specific energy; correlation: SNR accuracies manipulated to approximate filtering, normal draws + ground-truth means, constant per-sensor bias at start + residual network bias in covariance (SPY-1B(V)/D-commensurate; CEC stds as Link-16 proxy — operational numbers unavailable); Link-16 req 8.7 mrad + 150 m; kill tests (launcher/prelaunch/inflight/uplink/handover/lethality) geometry-independent but RV-in-track-dependent (geometric dependence planned) [P3, pp.4-8; companions Shafer/Bates/McDonald].

## Assumptions
- Idealizations (simulation-only by design): hemispheric search, perfect tracks/correlation, unlimited comms, Pk=1, ground-truth clustering, geometry-independent kills, local-track-only scheduling, single weapon/no salvo split, zero reengagement in scenario [P3, pp.5-8].
- Planning: RP&A constant RCS; extended mission at special auto reaction; sectors for planned pairs only (Fig.2 yellow); enlarged assets; CEC bias proxy; registration benefit assumed [P3, pp.6-8].
- Operational: coordination-time guarantees; 2 msgs/slot; 5 discriminations; soft/tolerance/group constants; priority order; 1-s decisions; CEC AAW-only; 4 ships illustrative [P3, pp.4-6].
- Scenario: DICE 6-DOF truth; 10-s impact spread/Monte Carlo; Fig.2 engageability; saturated raid (simultaneous discriminations x shooters x depth = threats) so overengagement -> free rider [P3, pp.6, 8-9].

## Metrics & results
- Ideal [P3, pp.9-10, Figs.4-5]: free fire almost always 4-5 free riders (only 2/6 to Asset 24 engaged -> >=4 guaranteed; 2 units x 4/5 to Asset 7 -> 1 possible + 3-4 overengagements; 6-threat cases add overengagement). Sectored worse than free fire (Unit1 all 4, Unit2 misses 2/7, Unit3 misses 4/9 -> mass at 6, smaller at 5 when 6 shots). Sectored zero overengagements (perfect knowledge + static). Free fire high overengagements. First launch far fewer (only near-simultaneous launches before status/commit). DED seldom (3 cases dual-track: unit never locally tracked No Dong booster so RV couldn't inherit remote number). First launch better than free/sectored via status freeing capacity; still free riders (Units1,4 <5 engageable after 2,3 fire first). DED best, nearly perfect (early detection >=40s before first launch; pre-launch pairing solves latency; occasional sequential-algorithm free riders).
- Achievable [P3, pp.10-13, Figs.6-13]: generally worse (fewer/later detections, multiple tracks, miscorrelations). Free fire ~same as ideal, some runs 3 free riders (fewer detections reordered intercepts). Fig.6: 84.1% +/-0.5% (1-sigma) detected in time; 708 objects undetected by planned unit (of 4500 stated); RP&A 95% >=20s goal failed (RCS too high), extended mission chosen. Sectored ~same as ideal (assigned assets -> no track-error confusion -> zero overengagements; typically 5-6 free riders, occasionally 4 when 2,3 get 6 shots). First launch worse than ideal, overengagements much higher; 96% from multiple track numbers for same object (air-picture product, not scheme); remainder late status (near-simultaneous). Free riders not much higher (overengaging units hadn't hit capacity in ideal). DED much worse than ideal; 88% overengagements from multiple tracks. Fig.9: goal 1 track/20 events never; often <25 but avg 28 remotes (1.3/object with remote); >=1 booster reported (someone not tracking RV). Fig.10 miscorrelations rarer. Fig.11 Run 24: cyan in-track, yellow miscorrelations, magenta remotes; decorrelations after first launch 407s before first intercept 469s; post-intercept track degrade -> further decorrelations. Fig.12: launches before all objects detected — cannot guarantee correct solution without complete info. DED more launches (Units1,4 nearly always 5 targets) -> more missile failures -> "engagement terminated" re-decisions counted as overengagement (future: separate from unintentional). DED others more available via load-leveling. Fig.13 leakers > free riders (fallible missiles); only 1/400 runs zero leakers (DED).
- Conclusions [P3, pp.12-13]: ideal DED significantly better, nearly perfect; realistic shortcomings blur DED vs first-launch gap. By free riders: sectored worst, closely followed by free fire; two dynamic significantly better, modeled DED > first launch. Achievable picture poor (1.3 remotes/object, miscorrelations, undetected planned targets); latency + changing launch times seemed less effect than picture quality.

## Baselines & comparisons
- Free fire = uncoordinated lower bound. Sectored = static unbidded no-comms (minimal overengagement, fragile to uneven raids, wasted capacity). First launch = dynamic unbidded launch-message deconfliction (mutual aid, easy, but capability-blind farthest-first, latency/picture-sensitive). DED = bidded pre-launch optimization (best ideal via 8-test sieving + grouping/freezing/ordering; retains achievable edge but gap narrows on correlation/multi-track errors). Dimensions: free riders, unintentional overengagements (+ reliability reengagements conflated in DED), leakers (achievable), detection-to-launch margins, remote-track counts.

## Limitations
- Authors stated future work [P3, p.13]: achievable radar/correlation excursions; other theaters/raids/locations; other schemes; more systems (NTW, Patriot, THAAD); competing AAW/OCMD for resources. Plus: ground-truth clustering, local-only scheduling, single weapon/no split, geometry-independent lethality, CEC-bias proxy, failure reengagements miscounted, decorrelation-no-recorrelation.
- Identified: ideal non-physical by design; achievable still CEC-bias, enlarged assets, planned-pair-tuned search, 5-discrimination estimate, coordination guarantees; no EA, no maneuver/penaids beyond separable RV/booster, no mixed load, no human delays beyond latencies; 10-s compressed raid drives saturation; single vignette + 100 runs limits tails (95% CI shown, values untabulated); counting method undescribed; seeds/RNG, DICE/RP&A inputs, 20-C2+36-fighter load not exercised as shooters; Figs lack tables (visual read); overengagement conflates coordination failure vs reliability reengagement in DED achievable.
